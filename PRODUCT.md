# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two equally-weighted audiences, neither subordinate to the other:

**Families** — parents (ages 28–55) seeking faith-aligned programming for their children. They are mobile-first, community-oriented, and respond to warmth, authenticity, and a sense of belonging. They arrive via social media or word of mouth. They want to quickly understand: "Is this for us? Where is the nearest event? How do I host one?"

**Grant funders / program officers** — institutional reviewers at foundations, government agencies, or corporate giving programs. They evaluate organizational credibility on a 90-second scan. They look for: named leadership, EIN, 990s, impact metrics with dates, theory of change, and evidence of operational maturity. They do not respond to marketing; they respond to evidence.

## Product Purpose

Brave for Good (braveforgood.org) is the nonprofit home for a grassroots movement of faith-centered, character-building story hours hosted in public libraries. The site's primary jobs: (1) convert institutional funders into grant partners by projecting credibility, transparency, and impact; (2) convert community members into hosts and attendees for the See You at the Library program. Success = a foundation officer says "this org is grant-ready" and a parent says "I want to take my kids to this."

## Brand Personality

Trustworthy. Courageous. Enduring.

Voice: clear, grounded, plain-spoken American — not political, not preachy, not corporate. Reads like a well-edited letter from a serious person. Confident without being loud.

## Reference Aesthetic

Classic Americana — the visual language of national park posters, WPA-era typography, Audubon-style illustration, and the best American editorial design traditions. Timeless rather than contemporary. Strong serif-led hierarchy. Heritage palette used with restraint. Feels like something that will look as right in 20 years as it does today.

## Anti-references

- **Megachurch website**: over-produced video-loop heroes, celebrity-pastor centerfold, emotional-manipulation design, neon cross accents.
- **Homeschool co-op flyer**: clip art, Comic Sans energy, low-fidelity, scattered visual hierarchy.
- **SaaS nonprofit**: gradient cards, dashboard metric blocks, "impact by the numbers" hero-metric templates, glassmorphism.
- **Political campaign site**: aggressive red/white/blue, rally-poster energy, urgency-at-all-costs design.

## Design Principles

1. **Type carries the weight.** In the Classic Americana tradition, typography IS the design. Hierarchy, structure, and emotion come from type — not from gradients, icons, or decorative shapes.
2. **Evidence before decoration.** Every design choice must earn its place by communicating something real. No element exists purely for visual interest.
3. **Institutional gravity for funders; human warmth for families.** The same page must do both. Achieve this through content sequencing and tonal variation, not visual splitting.
4. **The movement, not the celebrity.** Kirk Cameron and others are named participants, not the brand. Design choices center the community story, not individual speakers.
5. **Enduring over contemporary.** Avoid anything that will read as dated in three years: gradient meshes, oversized decorative blobs, motion-heavy scroll effects, neon accents, brutalist revival grids.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 Level AA. All pages must pass: sufficient color contrast (4.5:1 text, 3:1 UI), keyboard navigation, semantic HTML landmarks, meaningful alt text for all images, no autoplay media, captions required for any video content. Reduced motion support via `prefers-reduced-motion`.
